The problem

If you run social media for small businesses, you know this scene. The posts go into a WhatsApp group as screenshots. Feedback comes back as voice notes and circled images, buried under tomorrow's chat. A week later nobody can say which version the client actually approved — and the wrong one goes out. The tools that fix this charge per client: Planable's own help page lists $39 per workspace per month, and the "required approval" setting only starts at the $59 plan. A five-client micro-agency pays agency-sized money for one small feature. On G2, a verified small-business reviewer says of Sprout Social — $79 a seat — that it has no way to seek client approval inside the platform at all, so the team builds the calendar in one tool and schedules by hand in another.

What Manzuri does instead

One link per client, sent where the conversation already lives. The client opens it on their phone — no account, no password — sees the month's posts in order, and taps Approve or Request changes (with one structured question: the image, the caption, or both?). Every decision is stamped with its time and locked to the version they saw. If you edit a post after approval, the approval is voided — visibly, with the history kept — so an approved version can never quietly become a different post. When the month is done, one click exports the checklist of only-approved posts, ready to schedule.

Honest limits

  • No auto-posting. Manzuri ends at approval + checklist. Publishing still happens in your scheduler — that is deliberate: social-platform posting permissions are a separate, heavier gate.
  • The link is the key. Anyone holding a client's link can approve as that client. Links are long and unguessable; treat them like keys, one per client.
  • Built for micro-agencies. A handful of clients, a month of posts at a time. Not a media-buying suite, not analytics.
